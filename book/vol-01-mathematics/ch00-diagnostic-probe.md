# Vol 01 · Ch 0 — The Foundation Probe

> **Purpose.** Find the true floor of your mathematical intuition — not what you can
> compute, but what you can *see*. Every later intuition (variance as area, gradients
> as slopes, probability as counting, attention as weighted averages) rests on the
> pictures tested here. We are not guessing where the floor is. We are measuring it.

---

## §0 — Rules of the probe

Read all of this before question 1.

1. **Untimed.** Take the whole day if needed. Breaks are fine. Rushing is not.
2. **Paper and pencil only.** No calculator, no phone, no internet, no Python, no
   asking anyone. The point is to find what is *in your head*.
3. **Every answer has three parts:**
   - **A** — the answer.
   - **W** — *why*, in one or two sentences or a drawing. "Because that's the rule"
     is not a why. If you cannot say why, write "no why" — that is honest and useful.
   - **C** — confidence, one digit:
     - `1` = guessing
     - `2` = think so, could be wrong
     - `3` = certain, could teach it to a child
4. **Do not look anything up, before or after.** A wrong answer is data. A looked-up
   answer is a lie to the diagnosis, and the person it hurts is you.
5. **Do not skip a question because it looks childish.** The childish ones are the
   foundation. If the answer is obvious, it costs you ten seconds.
6. **Stop rule per strand.** If you get three questions in a row in a strand with
   `C = 1` and no why, write "FLOOR" and move to the next strand. We have found what
   we needed there.
7. **Traps exist.** Some questions are designed around common misconceptions. If a
   question feels too easy, still write the why. Confidence-3 wrong answers are the
   most important results in this entire probe — they are the "faked knowledge" you
   said you want gone.
8. **Draw whenever a question says draw.** Dots, boxes, number lines, grids. Ugly is
   fine. Missing is not.
9. Number each answer exactly as here (`A3`, `F12`). Photograph every page.
   Save to `exercises/vol-01/probe/`. Commit with subject
   `Add foundation probe answers, strands A-P`. Then send me the photos or a typed
   transcript.

How results are read (so you know there is no trick):

| Result | Meaning | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Right, C=3, good why | solid | none |
| Right, C=3, no why | procedure without picture | medium |
| Right, C=1 or 2 | lucky / shaky | medium |
| Wrong, C=1 | honest gap | high |
| **Wrong, C=3** | **false knowledge** | **highest** |

The floor is set **one level below the lowest strand where anything is "high" or
"highest".** We then rebuild upward, picture-first, and re-probe.

## §1 — Strand A · Counting and the number line

**A1.** Draw a number line from 0 to 10. Mark 3 and 8. How many steps from 3 to 8?
Count them on your drawing.

**A2.** On the same line, how many steps from 8 to 3? Is that the same number as A1?
Is anything different about the two journeys?

**A3.** What does `8 − 3` *mean* on the number line? (Not the answer — the meaning.)

**A4.** What does `3 − 8` mean on the number line? Where do you land?

**A5.** Put these in order, smallest first: `7, 0, 12, 3, 9`. Then say: is 0 a number,
or "nothing"? Why?

**A6.** Two towns are at kilometre-markers 42 and 17 on a highway. How far apart are
they? Draw it.

**A7.** What is the *distance* between 5 and 5?

**A8.** Without computing, which is bigger: `1000 − 1` or `1000 − 10`? Why, using a
picture?

## §6 — Strand F · What multiplication and division *are*

**F1.** Draw `3 × 4` as a grid of dots. Now draw `4 × 3`. Count both. Why are they
equal — what do you *see*?

**F2.** Draw `2 × 5` as a box of unit squares. How many squares? Which two things did
you multiply, and where are they *in the picture*?

**F3.** What is `12 ÷ 3` in the "sharing" meaning (split 12 into 3 equal groups)?
Draw it.

**F4.** What is `12 ÷ 3` in the "measuring" meaning (how many 3s fit in 12)? Draw it.
Same answer — different picture. Describe the difference in one sentence.

**F5.** `12 ÷ 4 = 3`. Draw a 12-square rectangle with one side 4. What is the other
side? What does division have to do with rectangles?

**F6.** What is `7 × 0`? What is `0 ÷ 7`? What is `7 ÷ 0`? For the last one, use the
"how many fit" meaning to explain what goes wrong.

**F7.** `6 × 7 = 42`. Without recomputing, what is `6 × 8`? What is `6 × 70`?
What is `60 × 70`?

**F8.** `15 × 12`. Split 12 into `10 + 2`. Draw a rectangle 15 wide, split into a
15×10 part and a 15×2 part. Add the two areas.

**F9.** `23 × 17` — do it as a 4-part rectangle (`20+3` by `10+7`). Write the four
areas and their sum.

**F10.** What does `a × b` mean if `a = 2.5` and `b = 4`? Draw a rectangle. Is
"repeated addition" still a good picture? Is "area" still a good picture?

**F11.** `100 ÷ 0.5`? Why is the answer 200 — use a picture.

**F12.** If `x × 5 = 35`, what is `x`? What operation undoes multiplying by 5, and
*why* does it undo it?

## §7 — Strand G · Length, area, perimeter, units

**G1.** A rectangle is 3 cm wide and 4 cm tall. Draw it on a cm grid. What is its
*perimeter*? Walk around it and count.

**G2.** Same rectangle. What is its *area*? Count the squares. Why is the answer in
"cm²" and the perimeter in "cm"?

**G3.** What does "cm²" mean, literally? What does "1 square centimetre" look like?

**G4.** Two rectangles both have perimeter 12 cm. Draw one that is 1×5 and one that
is 3×3. Which has more area? So does perimeter tell you area?

**G5.** A square has area 25 cm². What is its side? What is the *name* of the
operation you just did?

**G6.** A square has side 7. Area? A square has side `s`. Area? Why is "s squared"
called *squared*?

**G7.** Double every side of a 2×3 rectangle. How does the perimeter change? How does
the area change? Draw both. Why are the two answers different?

**G8.** A triangle has base 6 and height 4. Draw it inside a 6×4 rectangle. What
fraction of the rectangle is the triangle? So what is its area?

**G9.** If I tell you a room is "20", is that a length, an area, or unknowable? Why
do units matter?

**G10.** Convert 2 m to cm. Convert 2 m² to cm². Why are the two conversion factors
different?

## §12 — Strand L · Averages and simple data

**L2.** Mean of `5, 5, 5, 5`? Mean of `0, 0, 0, 20`? Same mean? Same data?

**L3.** Five people have mean salary 100. One person leaves who earned 100. New

**L4.** Five people have mean salary 100. One person joins earning 700. New mean?

**L5.** The mean of 4 numbers is 10. Three of them are `7, 12, 9`. Fourth?

**L6.** Median of `3, 9, 1, 7, 5`? Median of `3, 9, 1, 7, 5, 100`? Why does the

**L7.** For `2, 4, 9` (mean 5): write each value's *distance* from the mean. Which
direction is each one? Add the *signed* differences. What do you get? Will that
always happen?

**L8.** Same data. Square each difference. Add. Divide by 3. What did you just
compute? What is the square root of it, roughly?
